# Amazon Main Image Requirements 2026: Complete Seller Guide

*Last reviewed: August 28, 2026*

Your Amazon MAIN image has two jobs: **meet Amazon's image requirements** and **make the product immediately understandable in a crowded search result**.

For most standard product listings, that means a pure white background, an accurate representation of the product, enough resolution for a clear image, and a product that fills most of the frame. Amazon also prohibits added text, logos, watermarks, confusing props, and other elements that can misrepresent what the customer is buying.

**But compliance is only the floor.**

A technically acceptable image can still be too small, visually weak, difficult to understand on a phone, or almost identical to the products sitting next to it.

This guide covers the current **[Amazon main image requirements](/service/listing-optimization)**, the technical specifications worth checking before upload, common mistakes, the latest AI image considerations, and the practical steps that can turn a compliant image into a stronger search result.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Important:** Amazon's image requirements can vary by marketplace and category. When a category-specific style guide applies, check that guidance before publishing the image.

## Amazon Main Image Requirements at a Glance

Use this as your pre-upload checklist:

| Requirement | Practical Target |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Background** | Pure white, RGB `255, 255, 255` / `#FFFFFF` |
| **Product Fill** | At least **85%** of the frame |
| **Product Authenticity** | True-to-life depiction of the physical item being sold |
| **Visibility** | Product should be clear and easy to identify |
| **Text and Graphics** | No added text, promotional graphics, borders, or badges |
| **Logos and Watermarks** | No added logos or watermarks |
| **Props and Accessories** | Do not include items that are not part of the purchase |
| **Image Quality** | Sharp, professional, well lit, without visible pixelation or jagged edges |
| **Dimensions** | 1,000 px minimum on longest side; **1,600+ px is the safer production target** |
| **Resolution Metadata** | At least **72 dpi** in Amazon's current guidance |
| **File Size Limit** | Keep at or below Amazon's current upload limit (commonly listed as **10 MB** in Seller Central) |
| **File Formats** | **JPEG preferred**; PNG, TIFF, and GIF are also supported in Amazon guidance |
| **Color Profile** | **RGB / sRGB preferred** for web delivery |

Amazon's 2026 product photography guidance lists JPEG as preferred, 1,000 pixels as the minimum on the longest side, 1,600+ as optimal, 72 dpi, pure white RGB 255/255/255, and 85% to 100% product fill. A current Seller Central response also lists a 10 MB file-size limit.

### A Note About Image Resolution
You may see different Amazon pages reference different resolution thresholds.

Current Amazon photography guidance lists 1,000 pixels as the minimum and 1,600+ pixels as optimal, while other Amazon guidance has historically used 1,600 pixels in relation to zoom.

For production work, there is little benefit to designing right at the lower limit:
* **Use 1,600 pixels or larger on the longest side whenever practical.**
* That gives you a stronger source file and avoids having to enlarge a weak image later.

## What Is an Amazon MAIN Image?

The **MAIN image** is the primary product image shown to customers in Amazon search results and on the product detail page.

It is not supposed to explain everything about the product. Its job is much simpler:

> **Show the shopper exactly what the product is, clearly and accurately.**

Amazon says the MAIN image should show only the product for sale on a white background, with the product filling at least 85% of the frame.

That is why sellers often make a mistake here. They try to turn the MAIN image into an infographic.

Save the detailed selling points for the rest of the gallery. The MAIN image should make the product easy to recognize first.

## The Baseline: Amazon's Main Image Requirements

### 1. Use a Pure White Background

For a standard MAIN image, Amazon specifies a pure white background:

* **RGB:** `255, 255, 255`
* **HEX:** `#FFFFFF`

A studio backdrop can look white while still containing:

* Gray gradients
* Warm or cool tint
* Uneven lighting
* Background spill
* Edge halos
* Off-white pixels

That is why the final exported file matters more than how the physical backdrop looked during the shoot.

#### Check the Background at Pixel Level

Do not rely only on your eyes. Sample a few areas of the final export, especially around the product edges. Representative background areas should read:

`R = 255, G = 255, B = 255`

Poor masking and compression can leave slightly off-white pixels even when the image looks white on your monitor.

For production work, a clean clipping path or carefully controlled mask is often more reliable than simply pushing exposure until the background disappears.

---

### 2. Make the Product Fill at Least 85% of the Frame

Amazon says the product should occupy **at least 85% of the image frame**.

This sounds simple, but it is easy to get wrong. A 2,000-pixel image with a tiny product in the middle is still a weak MAIN image.

Think about what the shopper sees in the search thumbnail. Ask:

> *“Can I identify the product immediately?”*

Do not crop the product aggressively just to hit a percentage. You still need to show the product completely where the applicable category rules require it and preserve its proportions.

**The goal:** Use the available frame efficiently without making the product feel cramped.

---

### 3. Show the Actual Product

Amazon's current image guidance says the MAIN image should accurately represent the product being sold and describes the MAIN image as a professional photograph of the actual product. Amazon also says illustrations and other representations that do not accurately depict the product are not appropriate for the MAIN image.

The image should accurately communicate the real:

* Shape & proportions
* Color & finish
* Physical size
* Quantity & pack count
* Configuration & included components

Do not make the product look materially different simply because a more dramatic version would look better. **Accuracy matters more than visual drama.**

---

### 4. Do Not Add Promotional Text or Graphics

Avoid overlays such as:

* *"Best Seller"* or *"Amazon's Choice"*
* *"20% OFF"* or promotional pricing
* *"100% Organic"* or certification stamps
* *"Free Shipping"*
* Arrows, bursts, or decorative borders
* Promotional badges, watermarks, added logos, or extra text

Amazon's MAIN image guidance prohibits added text, logos, borders, color blocks, watermarks, and other graphics over the product or in the background.

Those selling points belong in the appropriate secondary image or listing content when allowed.

---

### 5. Do Not Add Props That Are Not Part of the Purchase

A product can look more premium when photographed with accessories or decorative objects.

The problem is that the customer may assume those items are included. Amazon's guidance says the MAIN image should show only the product for sale and not unrelated props or accessories.

A simple rule works well:

> **If the customer is not buying it, keep it out of the MAIN image.**

That is especially important for kits, bundles, furniture, tools, and products commonly shown with accessories.

---

### 6. Keep the Image Sharp

Amazon's current image guidance says images should not be blurry, pixelated, or have jagged edges.

A large file does not automatically mean a high-quality file. You can still end up with a poor image because of:

* A weak source photograph
* Excessive enlargement / digital upscaling
* Aggressive JPEG compression
* Poor background removal
* Over-sharpening artifacts
* Soft focus
* Rough masking

A 2,000-pixel file made from a poor source is still a poor source.

## Color Space: Export for the Web

Amazon technical guidance has referenced both RGB and CMYK, while noting RGB as the preferred option for display on the Amazon website.

For practical web production:

* **Export the final image in sRGB whenever possible.**

This is especially important when the product has brand-critical colors such as:

* Cosmetics & skincare packaging
* Clothing & footwear
* Consumer electronics
* Packaged food & supplements
* Branded lifestyle accessories

Check the final export rather than assuming the colors will remain identical after conversion. The goal is not just to make an image technically acceptable. The product shown to the customer should still look like the product they receive.

## What the 85% Rule Actually Means

The 85% rule is sometimes misunderstood as *"Crop everything until the product almost touches the edges."*

That is not the point. You still need to:

* Show the complete product where required
* Preserve its proportions
* Avoid accidental cropping of vital edges
* Keep important components visible
* Leave enough room for a clean composition
* Make the product easy to recognize

The requirement should influence your composition, not ruin it. For example, forcing a long narrow product into an aggressive crop just to make it appear larger can make the image harder to understand.

**The better target:** Maximum useful product presence without sacrificing accuracy or clarity.

## MAIN Image vs. Secondary Listing Images

One of the most common mistakes is asking the MAIN image to do everything. It should not.

Amazon recommends additional images that show different sides of the product, the product in use, and other details that are not visible in the primary view.

A useful gallery strategy is:

| Image Slot | Primary Job |
| :--- | :--- |
| **MAIN Image** | Identify the product and compete for the click |
| **Image 2** | Communicate the biggest benefit |
| **Image 3** | Explain important features & materials |
| **Image 4** | Show the product in real-world use |
| **Image 5** | Address objections, specifications, or dimensional scale |
| **Image 6+** | Add comparisons, social proof, use cases, or supporting details |

The exact sequence depends on the product. The principle is what matters:

> **Keep the MAIN image simple. Let the rest of the gallery do the explaining.**

## Beyond Compliance: Why White Backgrounds Are Harder Than They Look

A white background sounds easy until the product is:

* Glossy or polished
* Metallic or chrome
* Reflective
* Transparent or clear glass
* Pure white or pale pastel
* Heavily textured
* Wrapped in reflective poly-packaging

The result you want is:

**Clean White Background + Accurate Product + Controlled Highlights + Sharp Edges + Believable Depth**

That is why professional product image production often involves lighting control, background removal, 3D rendering, retouching, color correction, and careful edge work.

## Can Amazon Main Images Have Shadows or Reflections?

This is one area where it is better not to use a blanket rule.

Amazon's general guidance focuses on a pure white background and a clear, accurate representation of the product. It also prohibits unrelated visual elements that can confuse the customer.

* **Avoid:** Intentionally adding a dramatic studio floor, colored reflection, simulated environment, or heavy shadow to the MAIN image just because it looks more premium.
* **Allowed:** A natural photographic contact shadow underneath the product that preserves the required white background while anchoring the product to prevent it from floating.

For unusual products or categories, check the relevant category guidance before uploading.

## Can You Use 3D Rendering or AI-Generated Images for an Amazon Main Image?

This is an area where sellers should be careful.

### 3D Product Rendering

Amazon's current general image guidance describes the MAIN image as a professional photograph of the actual product. It also says the image should accurately represent the item being sold.

Amazon's policy does not clearly provide a universal approval statement saying that every photorealistic 3D render is acceptable as a MAIN image.

So the lower-risk production approach is:

* **MAIN Image:** Use an actual product photograph for the MAIN image when possible.
* **Secondary Images & A+ Content:** Leverage [3D product rendering](/service/listing-optimization) extensively for exploded views, technical cutaways, comparison graphics, packaging visuals, and controlled lifestyle scenes.

Whatever method you use, the final image must accurately represent the real product. Do not use rendering or AI to invent product features, colors, quantities, components, packaging details, or physical characteristics that the customer will not actually receive.

---

### AI-Generated People: An Important 2026 Requirement

Amazon now requires specific metadata for product images and A+ Content that contain photorealistic AI-generated people. Sellers must use an IPTC-compatible metadata editor and add:

`contains-synthetic-performer`

to the **`dc:subject` (XMP)** field before uploading the media.

Amazon states this requirement applies when the image or video contains photorealistic AI-generated people. It does **not** apply when:

* The image contains no people (product-only images)
* The people are real, even if AI tools were used to edit or retouch them
* The people are not photorealistic (e.g. vector illustrations)
* The image contains specified fictional characters from expressive works such as films or games

#### Practical AI Workflow:

**Create/Edit Image ➔ Export File ➔ Add `contains-synthetic-performer` to XMP ➔ Verify Metadata ➔ Upload to Seller Central**

For a product-only image with no photorealistic AI-generated person, this particular metadata requirement does not apply. Because Amazon's policies can change, recheck Seller Central whenever you create new AI-assisted listing assets.

## 5 Costly Main Image Mistakes

1. **Adding Text or Promotional Badges:** A badge may look helpful, but the MAIN image is normally not the place for it. Move the information into the appropriate secondary images.
2. **Showing Items the Customer Does Not Receive:** An accessory can make the composition more attractive while creating the wrong expectation about what comes in the box, causing return spikes.
3. **Making the Product Too Small:** A huge white canvas with a tiny product meets the image dimensions while producing a weak search thumbnail.
4. **Uploading a Blurry or Poorly Compressed Source:** Increasing pixel dimensions does not repair weak photography. Fix the source instead of simply enlarging it.
5. **Trying to Show Every View in the MAIN Image:** Front, side, back, detail, features, and lifestyle context do not all belong in the primary image. Use the gallery.

## Mobile-First Optimization: The Hidden Image Test

Amazon shopping happens across desktop and mobile environments, so an image that looks excellent on a large monitor can become much weaker when it is reduced to a small search thumbnail. Sponsored Products, for example, can appear on both desktop and mobile browsers and in the Amazon mobile app.

The solution is not to put more information into the MAIN image: **make the existing information easier to see.**

### The Three-Second Squint Test

Take your final MAIN image and view it as a small thumbnail on your phone. Ask:
1. *Can I identify the product immediately?*
2. *Can I tell what makes it different?*
3. *Can I recognize the important physical characteristics without zooming in?*

If the answer is no, inspect:

* Product scale
* Framing & orientation
* Contrast & lighting
* Edge separation & overall clarity

The MAIN image is not a brochure. It is a small product signal competing with other small product signals.

## How to Audit Your Main Image Against Competitors

Compliance answers: *"Does the image follow Amazon's rules?"*  
Optimization asks: **"Would this image make me stop and look at the product?"**

Search your main Amazon keyword and look at the products around yours. Compare:

| Audit Factor | What to Check |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Compliance** | Does the image satisfy Amazon's applicable rules? |
| **Search Relevance** | Does the product clearly match the search intent? |
| **Product Scale** | Does your image use the frame effectively without clipping? |
| **Recognition** | Can you identify the product category instantly? |
| **Differentiation** | Does it look distinct from surrounding competitor products? |
| **Clarity** | Is the product understandable at thumbnail size? |
| **Offer Signals** | How do price, coupon, and delivery badge compare? |
| **Social Proof** | How do rating and review count compare? |

Then ask yourself:

> **“Why would someone click my product instead of the one immediately beside it?”**

If you cannot answer that question, you have found a useful optimization opportunity.

## Does a Better Main Image Lower ACOS?

Be careful with this idea. A stronger image can potentially help relevant shoppers understand and evaluate your product more quickly. That can influence the quality of traffic you generate, but there is no simple formula that says:

**Better Image ➔ Lower CPC ➔ Lower ACOS**

Amazon recommends looking at impressions, clicks, CPC, sales, and other campaign metrics together rather than using one metric in isolation. (Read our deep dive on [what is a good Amazon click-through rate](/blog/amazon-click-through-rate) to see benchmark ranges across ad types).

A better way to think about the funnel is:

**Relevant Impressions ➔ Clicks ➔ Detail Page Traffic ➔ Conversion ➔ Sales & Advertising Efficiency**

If a new image increases CTR but attracts visitors who do not convert, the change may not improve the business result. If CTR and conversion both improve, you have much stronger evidence that the creative change was valuable.

That is why main image optimization should be treated as part of the wider listing and advertising funnel.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What are Amazon's main image requirements for 2026?

Amazon's current photography guidance calls for a pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255), accurate product representation, at least 85% product fill, clear professional quality, and no added text, logos, watermarks, or unrelated props. Current Amazon guidance references 1,000 pixels as a minimum on the longest side and 1,600+ as optimal. Category-specific rules can add further requirements.

### What size should an Amazon main image be?

Amazon's current guidance references 1,000 pixels on the longest side as the minimum in its 2026 photography guidance, with 1,600+ described as optimal. For production, 1,600 pixels or larger is a sensible target whenever practical to support zoom functionality.

### Can Amazon main images contain text?

Normally, no. Amazon's general MAIN image guidance prohibits added text, logos, borders, color blocks, watermarks, and other graphics. Text that is naturally part of the physical product or packaging can appear as part of the product itself.

### Can Amazon main images have shadows or reflections?

Keep the MAIN image on a pure white background and avoid effects that introduce a visible environment or unrelated visual elements. A natural photographic contact shadow can sometimes be compatible with the requirements, provided the final image still meets the applicable white-background and product-presentation rules. For unusual products, check the applicable category-specific guidance before uploading.

### Can I use a 3D render as my Amazon main image?

Amazon's general MAIN image guidance describes the primary image as a professional photograph of the actual product. Amazon does not provide a blanket statement that all 3D renders are approved for the MAIN slot. For the lowest compliance risk, use an actual product photograph for the MAIN image when possible. 3D renders remain useful for secondary listing images, A+ Content, technical visuals, and other approved creative applications.

### Are AI-generated product images banned on Amazon?

There is no blanket rule in current Amazon guidance saying that every AI-generated product image is banned. Amazon does require `contains-synthetic-performer` metadata for listing images and A+ Content containing photorealistic AI-generated people. That requirement does not apply to product-only images with no photorealistic AI-generated people.

### What color profile should I use for Amazon product images?

Use RGB or sRGB for your final web export whenever possible. Amazon technical guidance has referenced both RGB and CMYK, while noting RGB as the preferred option for web display to avoid color shifts.

### What happens if my main image does not meet Amazon's requirements?

Non-compliant imagery can create listing visibility and image suppression problems. Amazon's seller guidance recommends correcting the image so that it meets the applicable product image requirements, and category-specific requirements may also apply.
